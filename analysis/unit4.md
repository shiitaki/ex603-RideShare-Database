Part 1: Per-Producer Breakdown & Reflection (Task 4.1)
A Driver Operations Manager or Fleet Supervisor relies on the per-producer breakdown to coach individual drivers, enforce operational compliance, and conduct targeted interventions on performance outliers—such as Driver_06, whose combination of high event volume and elevated cancellation and dispute counts requires immediate review. However, relying solely on basic aggregates introduces critical blind spots: averages conceal distribution by smoothing over extreme variations and hiding whether performance is steady or skewed by rare outliers, while counts conceal who is counted by masking individual volume equity and masking whether high output reflects completed rides or a string of failed events. Building on the conditional counts and regional rollups from Task 4.2, leadership next needs visibility into statistical dispersion and percentiles (e.g., P50, P90) to evaluate true variance, longitudinal trends to track producer reliability over time, and root-cause failure categorizations (such as driver unavailability versus payment failures) linked directly to individual producers.

Task 4.2 Write-Up
Part A: Conditional Aggregation in One Pass
The single-pass conditional aggregation answers two fundamental performance questions side by side: What is the total volume of events handled by each producer? and How many of those total events resulted in a failure or cancellation for each producer? By calculating both metrics simultaneously without filtering out completed trips, leadership can immediately evaluate each producer's scale alongside their operational error rate.

Part B: Subtotals and Grand Totals via ROLLUP
The Regional Director relies on subtotals per region rather than granular detail rows in order to efficiently evaluate market-level performance and demand distribution without sifting through hundreds of individual driver records. Meanwhile, the platform-wide grand total supports macro-level executive decisions—such as global capital allocation, enterprise budgeting, and platform-wide policy benchmarks—that regional group rows alone cannot contextualize without knowing the scale of the entire business.

Part C: Two-Step Pipeline with a WITH Clause
The two-step aggregation pipeline answers the question: What is the baseline performance benchmark—specifically regarding average event volume, revenue per trip, and overall failure rate—among high-volume producers who meet or exceed our minimum activity threshold? By filtering out low-volume noise in the second stage, leadership establishes a clean, representative standard against which individual driver performance can be fairly evaluated.
